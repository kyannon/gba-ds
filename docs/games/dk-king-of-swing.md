What GBA DS does with this game. Each rule is a promise the profile keeps; the regression tests check them (the cases citing a rule are listed by `tools/regress.py --list dk-king-of-swing`). Change a rule here first when the behaviour is meant to change, then the profile, then the tests and goldens. Rule numbers are never reused: a dropped rule stays, marked *retired*.

Every game also follows the [general rules](README.md#general-rules).

The levels are climbs: DK swings up walls of pegs, and the camera only moves up and down. In a level the second screen shows the level above the game's, so the two screens are one tall one, and DK crosses from the game's screen to it as he climbs (DKKS-3, DKKS-4). It bends two of the philosophy's principles, on purpose.

## Rules

**DKKS-1. Logo.** The logo is the title's lettering: DK King of Swing with its crown and ™ (the sprites above the monkey, without PRESS START, the copyright line or the monkey, whose browns show between the letters), taken from power-on with START pressed through the health warning and the Nintendo and Paon screens. In Europe the language menu comes first (A: English, already chosen) and there is no health warning; the lettering is the USA's. In Japan the title comes by itself, with no health warning: its lettering, ぶらぶらドンキー with its ™, on a wooden plank, without the monkey below it.

**DKKS-2. Logo elsewhere.** The second screen shows the logo at half strength wherever the game isn't in a level: the title, the file select, the game mode menu, the world map, the Jungle Jam scenes, King K. Rool's fights (their arenas are one screen, with no DK object), the cutscenes and the menus. The game is on the top screen there, as in every game.

**DKKS-3. The level over both screens, live.** In a level (the tutorial's rooms and the world map's levels), the game stays as it draws itself, hearts and bananas included, on the bottom screen, and the top screen shows the 160 rows of the level above it, so the two screens are one tall screen: the pegs and bananas coming up, the sky above them, moving with the game's camera. A copy of the game, taken from yours every frame with its camera a screen higher and without the hearts and bananas, draws it, objects and all (bananas, the other pegs, DK when he's up there). The game itself is never changed. What the level has nowhere above its top (the tutorial's strip along the ground, a room's ceiling) is black, as the game leaves it; what the game keeps on its own screen (Cranky Kong's speech bubble) is only on the game's, never twice. The game is on the bottom screen, not the top, for as long as the level is up; the top screen's picture is the game's logo's place everywhere else (DKKS-2). Between two rooms (the tutorial's: the game fades out, loads, fades in and settles its camera for a dozen frames) the two screens stay the level's, black while the game is and the room fading in, the logo not coming back in between; the layout leaves for the logo only when the game leaves the levels for a menu or the map. The copy runs on a thread of its own (in the web player, a second worker), so the game's own frame doesn't wait for it: each frame the game hands over its state, the copy's picture comes back a frame behind (the web: a frame and a bit) and is moved with the game's camera to the frame on the screen, so a still object is exactly where the exact copy has it and a moving one is a frame late. A copy slower than the game draws a picture for fewer of its frames, each moved with the camera until the next. A picture the copy drew is the exact copy's picture of its frame, byte for byte. With one core, or no threads, the copy runs in step, the game waiting for it each frame; there a device that can't keep the game at 60 frames a second with the copy (its frames average over 18.5 ms) has the copy run every other frame, then every third, with the top screen's picture moved with the game's camera in between (what shows is the sprites above moving at 30 or 20 frames a second and the far layers a pixel off on the frames between), then not at all (the game on its own screen, DKKS-5's layout); it tries the faster pace again after 30 quiet seconds. *By hand: speed on the device (one more copy of the game running two frames each frame, half the pinball games' view), and the slower paces' pictures on a slow one.*

**DKKS-4. DK crosses to the top screen.** When DK climbs past the top of the game's screen, the camera having stopped at the top of the level, both screens hold still and he climbs on across the gap between them: for a moment his body is split, the part above the game's screen on the top one and the rest on the bottom one, then he's on the top screen, climbing on, with the pegs he left on the bottom one. Nothing is moved for it: each copy draws him where he is, and the game already leaves out the sprites that are off its screen.

**DKKS-5. One screen.** With one screen, the game plays as it is; `pinball_live = 0` (Pinball two-screen view: Off) turns the copy off too (the game on the top screen, the logo on the other). The setting's name is the pinball games'. `GBADS_CLIMB_INSTEP=1` runs the copy in step with the game, as with one core, to compare; `GBADS_CLIMB_PACE=1|2|3|4` (the tests' own: the copy in step every, every second, every third frame, or never) holds the pace the device's speed would pick there.

## Exceptions

DKKS-3 and DKKS-4 (the level over both screens, DK climbing from one to the other and split between them for a moment) are exceptions, as the pinball games' tall view is: to the second screen's principles (nothing split across the two screens, and not the character you're moving) and to its cost (a copy of the game running two frames each frame). `pinball_live = 0` is the way back.

## Known issues

- Rewinding (holding the rewind button): the top screen keeps the picture it had while the game screen goes back, and after a second and a half of it the game goes back to the top screen and the logo to the other until the button is let go. The tall view is back two frames after. Each rewound step starts the copy afresh, so it has nothing new to draw until the game runs forward again.

## Not covered yet

The end of a level (its goal and the score screen), a death by damage, Time Attack and Jungle Jam played through their menus, and multiplayer: not reached. The levels were entered from the world map (the first level) and by setting the game's scene and level from the file select; the tutorial's rooms, 45 levels at their start and at the camera's top stop, the boss and King K. Rool's rooms, and a death by the timer were looked at, and every level id the game has (`0x10` to `0x6F`) was played 300 frames in each release. Objects the game moves only near its own camera show in the copy as the game has them: wasps, bats, bananas and the exit sign sit right across the gap, but not every enemy was looked at. Europe in its other four languages (the logo is taken in English) is untried.
