What GBA DS does with this game. Each rule is a promise the profile keeps; the regression tests check them (the cases citing a rule are listed by `tools/regress.py --list final-fantasy-1-2-dawn-of-souls`). Change a rule here first when the behaviour is meant to change, then the profile, then the tests and goldens. Rule numbers are never reused: a dropped rule stays, marked *retired*.

Every game also follows the [general rules](README.md#general-rules) (GEN-1 to GEN-11).

The cartridge holds two games, Final Fantasy and Final Fantasy II (with its extra chapter, Soul of Rebirth), chosen on its Title Select screen, and a Bestiary for each. Each game has its own memory layout; the rules say which game they're for.

## Why these screens

Asked what the games would have put on a second GBA screen: the two things a player keeps opening while walking about are the world map (hold B and press SELECT) and START's menu, which shows the whole party at once (level, HP and MP), the gil and play time, the crystals (Final Fantasy) and where the party is. Both are the game's own screens, opened on the hidden copy and taken as they are, with the cursor left out. In a battle the second screen shows the party as it was just before (the owner's choice, Nov 16, 2026 for Final Fantasy, Nov 17 for Final Fantasy II: who's in the party and how they stand help more than the logo, even with HP and play time from before the fight). The world map is the Enlarged Map in both games, which the game draws across its whole screen under its windows: shown without the windows, it fills the second screen. START's menu is shown without its command list (the owner's choice, Nov 17, 2026), as the party screen.

## Rules

**FF-1. Logo.** The logo is the cartridge's title: its FINAL FANTASY I & II lettering (BG1) and the DAWN OF SOULS bar under it (BG3), without the crystal, the two heroes' pictures behind it, PRESS START or the copyright lines. In the Japanese release, FINAL FANTASY I・II with the ADVANCE bar and ファイナルファンタジー under it (BG1 to BG3). Europe's title comes after its language screen (English, A). The same logo for both games.

**FF-2. Outside the games.** On the cartridge's title, its Title Select screen and the Bestiary, the second screen shows the logo, any pictures from a game played before are dropped, and no captures are tried there. Told by neither game's memory being set up: each release keeps a pointer to each game's memory, set only while that game runs (USA 0x02000584 for Final Fantasy, 0x0200099C for Final Fantasy II; Europe 0x020004D0 and 0x0200080C; Japan 0x020004F8 and 0x02000904).

**FF-3. A game's own title and new game.** On a game's own title, its New Game, Load and naming screens and its opening scenes, nothing can be opened yet: the second screen keeps the logo until the first picture is taken (FF-4, FF-5). (Captures are tried there and fail: neither the map's nor the menu's windows come up.)

**FF-4. Final Fantasy: the world map.** Walking about on the world map (Final Fantasy's map number, the first byte of its memory, is 0), the second screen shows the Enlarged Map (on the hidden copy: hold B, then SELECT, then A on Enlarged Map unless the game already opens it that way, as it does once the player has used it) over the whole second screen: the game draws it at twice the Full Map's size across its whole screen on BG2, under its windows, and BG2 is shown alone with the party's marker (a pulsing blue sparkle, at its largest), without the hand cursors, the button icons or any window (the help window "Select a location to examine.", the command window, the button window: all prompts). The view is moved on the hidden copy (R + pad, as a player scrolls it) until the party's mark is in the middle of the screen: the mark the map's hand cursor points at (it opens on the party), found again every frame (the game hands its sprites round as marks scroll in and out of view, and following one sprite slot sometimes followed a parked vehicle's mark instead: fixed Nov 17, 2026); the world wraps round, so there's map all the way to every edge. It's taken again at every refresh, so the marker follows the party, on foot or aboard (the airship too: its marker where it flies). On the world map the second screen only ever shows the map: if the map doesn't open on the hidden copy (its buttons pressed mid-step can go unseen; they're tried up to three times), the last picture stays, never the menu. (Until Nov 16, 2026: the Full Map, at its own size in the middle of the second screen.)

**FF-5. Final Fantasy: the menu elsewhere.** Walking about anywhere else (towns, castles, dungeons; the game opens its map in some dungeons too, at least late in the game, but there the party's HP and MP matter more than where the dungeon is), the second screen shows the party screen: START's menu as FF-12 keeps it, the party (portrait, name, level, class, HP and MP), the gil and play time, the crystals and where the party is, without the command window (its place filled by the party window the game draws beneath it), so it's the same picture as in a battle. It's taken again at every refresh, and at once on entering another map. (Until Nov 17, 2026: the menu with its command list, the hand cursor left out.)

**FF-6. A menu or the map open in the game.** With START's menu, one of its screens or the world map open in the game, the second screen shows what it would walking about there (FF-4, FF-5): the hidden copy closes them first (B, as many times as it takes for START to bring the menu up, at most three).

**FF-7. Last picture stays.** In a conversation, a shop, an inn, a scene the story plays while the party is on the map, and between maps, where neither the map nor the menu opens, the last picture stays; before the first, the logo. START's menu is told by its windows' frames (the command window's lower edge and the party window's left one, the same in every release), the world map screen by the parchment's and the button window's.

**FF-8. Battles.** In a battle (either game), from its first frame to the end of its results, the second screen shows the party kept from walking about (FF-12): its HP, MP and play time are from before the battle and don't change during it. When no picture of this party has been kept (a state loaded straight into a battle, another file's party), the last picture stays: the logo if there's none yet. After a battle, the map or the menu is taken again at once. Told by 0x02000000 being 1 (0 walking about and in the menus), in either game and every release. (Until Nov 16, 2026: the logo in either game's battles; in Final Fantasy II's until Nov 17, 2026.)

**FF-9. Game screen untouched.** Nothing leaves the game screen.

**FF-10. Final Fantasy II: the party screen.** Walking about in Final Fantasy II and Soul of Rebirth, anywhere but the world map (FF-11), the second screen shows the party screen as in Final Fantasy (FF-5): START's menu, the party (portrait, name, HP and MP), where the party is, and the time, steps and gil, without the command window (Items, Magic...: its place filled by the party window the game draws beneath it), the same picture as in a battle (FF-12). It's taken again at every refresh. FF-6 to FF-9 hold as in Final Fantasy. (Until Nov 17, 2026: the menu with its command list, the hand cursor left out.)

**FF-11. Final Fantasy II: the world map.** Walking about on Final Fantasy II's world map (told by the map's tile set, the byte 0x1192 past its memory's base, being 7: 0x020030B2 in the USA release; also 7 with the map or START's menu open, 5 in the Rebel Hideout, 6 in towns, 8 in Pandaemonium, 2 in battles; seen in all three releases), the second screen shows the Enlarged Map over the whole second screen, as Final Fantasy's (FF-4): the hidden copy opens the map (B, then SELECT) and picks Enlarged Map unless it's already open that way; the map (BG2) alone with the game's own marks (the party's white circle, the towns' and vehicles' marks), without the hand cursors, the scroll arrows, the button icons or any window; scrolled (R + pad, two pixels a frame) until the party's mark, the one the map's hand cursor points at as it opens, is in the middle. In the Japanese release the game draws the Enlarged Map only where its windows leave room (left of x 128, below y 24: its command window is wider): there the hidden copy also scrolls the map 112 pixels right, then up 24, then back left, and fills the rest of the second screen from those pictures, each placed by how far the party's mark moved (about twice the frames of a USA capture). The marks blink in the game (a dot growing into a disc and back); the second screen shows them at one step, every time: the disc with a one-pixel black outline. Taken again at every refresh, and at once on reaching or leaving the world map; once the map has opened, never the party screen there, as in FF-4. Early in the game the map can't be opened yet (B and SELECT do nothing, as on leaving Altair the first time): until it has opened on the hidden copy for this party, the second screen shows the party screen on the world map too (FF-10), which also keeps the party for battles (FF-12). (Until Nov 17, 2026: the Full Map, 128 by 136 pixels in the middle of the second screen, its marks at their largest; and the world map was told by the byte 0x11C4 past the base being 4, which held only late in the game, so early on the second screen kept the hideout's picture.)

**FF-12. The party kept for battles.** While walking about (either game), the hidden copy also takes START's menu without showing it on the world map (every fourth capture there, and the first), and keeps the latest one: that's the picture FF-8 shows in a battle. It's START's menu with the command window at the top right left out: the game draws the party window across the whole screen under the right-hand windows (BG0 under BG1), so the menu's top rows (72 in Final Fantasy, 80 in Final Fantasy II) are taken without BG1, and the party window's own frame and colours fill where the commands were; the other windows stay (Final Fantasy: the gil and play time, crystals and location; Final Fantasy II: the location and the time, steps and gil). It's shown only for the party it was taken of, told by the four characters' names and classes (the first byte of Final Fantasy's memory + 0x7AC, then every 0x48 bytes, 12-byte names; in Japan every 0x44, 8-byte names), in Final Fantasy II the four names at the first byte of its memory + 0x12, then every 0x80 bytes (up to 12 bytes, ended by a 0; the USA release's, assumed in the others); these don't change in a battle: a battle never shows a party from another file. A new party on the world map is kept at the next capture, not the fourth. (Until Nov 17, 2026 it was dropped outside the games, which the web player, whose captures run apart from the game, never saw, so its battles showed the logo.)

**FF-13. Game over.** When the whole party falls in a battle (either game: every character's HP 0), the second screen shows the logo from that moment, and the pictures kept are dropped: through "The party was defeated.", the fade and the game's own title and its New Game or Load screens, until the first picture of the game taken up again (FF-3). (The owner's choice, Nov 17, 2026: the party screen of a party that's just been wiped out is no use.) Told in a battle (FF-8) by the party's HP in the game's battle memory: Final Fantasy's in a block it sets up for each battle, found by a word 0x7E8 bytes in (Japan: 0x7D8) that holds the block's own address, the four HPs from 0x100 in, one every 0x60 bytes (Japan: 0x5C), seen in all three releases; Final Fantasy II's at its memory's base + 0x7250 (Japan: 0x7240, Europe: 0x7274), one every 0x1F8 bytes (seen in all three releases). (Until Nov 17, 2026 Japan's was read at 0x7250, where it's always 0: every Final Fantasy II battle there showed the logo.) Final Fantasy II's opening battle, which the party loses by the story, shows the logo too (it does already: no party has been kept by then). A party all turned to stone, or out some other way that leaves HP above 0, isn't seen: the last picture stays until the game's title, and through it (its captures fail), until the next game is under way.

## Not covered yet

- **Final Fantasy's ship and canoe**: the cases walk the world map on foot and fly the airship; the map is expected to open aboard the others as well.
- **Soul of Rebirth's world map**: Soul of Rebirth's cases are in Machanon (the menu); FF-11 is expected to hold on its world map as in Final Fantasy II.
- **The European release in French, German, Spanish or Italian**: its windows are the same as in English; the cases play it in English.
- **Final Fantasy II in Europe and Japan**: the cases reach Final Fantasy's world map, Castle Cornelia and a game over in both, and Final Fantasy II's Rebel Hideout (the party screen, FF-10); Final Fantasy II's world map early in the game (the party screen), its battles and its game over are covered in both (Japan from the owner's state outside Altair, Europe by walking out of Altair: down from the town's tile (9, 22)); the late game too (the Enlarged Map: Europe's from a GameFAQs save, Japan's from that save rebuilt for the Japanese release); Final Fantasy II's menu there is expected to match the USA one (its frames are checked the same way), and its world map byte and party names to be the same distance past its base (FF-11, FF-12). A Final Fantasy II state from either would settle it; if the byte differs, the menu shows on the world map instead.

## How the states were made

All in the harness from power-on, on the USA ROM unless the case says otherwise (Europe and Japan: the same buttons, A on Europe's language screen first): Final Fantasy's new game (each character auto-named, SELECT), the opening on the world map outside Cornelia, Castle Cornelia's 1F, a battle with goblins near Cornelia; Final Fantasy II's new game (the default names), its opening battle with the Black Knights, the Rebel Hideout in Altair and Altair itself. The endgame states come from the owner's GameShark save (`saves/BFFE-endgame.sav`, converted with `tools/sharkport.py`), loaded in the app (the harness has no battery save) and played on in the harness: Final Fantasy's file 1 in the Chaos Shrine's past (Exit, then the airship); Final Fantasy II's file 1 in Pandaemonium 10F (Teleport, to the world map) and file 3, Soul of Rebirth in Machanon. `us-ff2-world-battle.ss`: from `us-ff2-world.ss`, LEFT and RIGHT for 30 frames each until a battle with Pit Fiends began (Nov 17, 2026). The game-over states (Nov 17, 2026): `us-ff1-weak-world.ss`, `us-ff1-world.ss` with Desh at 1 HP and the others fallen (HP 0, status 1 at +0x10 of their records); `us-ff1-defeat.ss`, from it, LEFT and RIGHT until a battle with goblins; `us-ff2-weak-pandaemonium.ss`, `us-ff2-pandaemonium.ss` with everyone at 1 HP (+0x22 of each record); `us-ff2-defeat.ss`, from it, LEFT and RIGHT until a battle with Fire Gigas. Europe's and Japan's (Nov 17, 2026): `eu-ff1-weak-world.ss` / `jp-ff1-weak-world.ss` and `eu-ff1-defeat.ss` / `jp-ff1-defeat.ss` made as the USA ones (Japan's records 4 bytes shorter: HP at +0x10, status at +0x0C); `eu-ff2-hideout.ss` / `jp-ff2-hideout.ss`, from power-on: Final Fantasy II's New Game with the default names, A through the opening, B out of the password prompt in the Rebel Hideout. Final Fantasy II early on the world map (Nov 17, 2026): `jp-ff2-world.ss`, the owner's, outside Altair; `jp-ff2-world-battle.ss`, from it, LEFT and RIGHT until hornets; `jp-ff2-weak-world.ss`, it with everyone at 1 HP; `jp-ff2-defeat.ss`, from that, a battle with hornets, each character attacking themselves (A, RIGHT, DOWN as needed, A); `us-ff2-early-world.ss`, the USA release just outside Altair (from the Rebel Hideout through the town, found by a search of its tiles: the exit is down from (9, 22)). Europe's Final Fantasy II outside Altair (Nov 18, 2026): `eu-ff2-world.ss`, from `eu-ff2-hideout.ss` through the town (a search of its tiles: down from (9, 22)); `eu-ff2-world-battle.ss`, from it, DOWN and UP until hornets; `eu-ff2-weak-world.ss`, it with everyone at 1 HP; `eu-ff2-defeat.ss`, from that, a battle in which Firion and Maria fell to their own attacks and Guy has just attacked himself. `eu-ff2-late-world.ss` (Nov 18, 2026): a GameFAQs save for Europe's release (in German; `tools/sharkport.py`, magic 83748340), Final Fantasy II's Load Game, file 2 (Mysidia): it opens on the world map beside Mysidia. (Its title menu has Load Game first when there are saves; Renaissance, below it, is Soul of Rebirth.) `jp-ff2-late-world.ss` (Nov 18, 2026): the same GameFAQs save rebuilt for the Japanese release (gba-ds-tests `saves/BFFJ-ff2-late.sav`), file 2. Final Fantasy II's save blocks are laid out alike in both releases, Japan's 2 bytes earlier: each block is the 32-byte title, a 4-byte check (the sum of the block's bytes after it, plus the block's length), then data; the three slots are 0xEB4 bytes from 0x4014 (Japan 0x4012), their summaries 0x22C from 0x6C30, then a 0x28 block and three 0x264 Soul of Rebirth blocks. Japan's own save (its title block and header) with Europe's blocks moved 2 bytes earlier and their checks made again: the game takes it as its own.
