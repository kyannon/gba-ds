What GBA DS does with this game. Each rule is a promise the profile keeps; the regression tests check them (the cases citing a rule are listed by `tools/regress.py --list mega-man-battle-network`). Change a rule here first when the behaviour is meant to change, then the profile, then the tests and goldens. Rule numbers are never reused: a dropped rule stays, marked *retired*.

Every game also follows the [general rules](README.md#general-rules) (GEN-1 to GEN-7).

How it fits the [philosophy](../../wiki/Philosophy.md): MegaMan's level, HP, armor and buster are shown outside a battle only on the PET's MegaMan screen, a few button presses away (principle 2: information the player would otherwise find in a menu); it's the game's own screen, with the PET menu's money counter, opened on the hidden copy (principles 3 and 4). Battles are left as the game plays them, on the game screen. Two exceptions (see Exceptions): the money counter and the MegaMan screen's panel put together in one frame made from the counter's own orange edge, as Final Fantasy Tactics Advance's turn order and Laws are; and on the net, a map of the area, which the game doesn't have.

## Rules

**MMBN-1. Logo.** On the title, on the game over screen (from the moment it comes up, MegaMan's status forgotten; until the eighth test build with maps, the status or the map stayed until the title), and until MegaMan's status is first taken (MMBN-2), the second screen shows the logo: the title's MEGAMAN BATTLE NETWORK lettering with its emblem, without the grid behind it, PRESS START or the copyright lines; in Japan the title's ロックマン エグゼ with its バトルネットワーク banner, its purple swoosh and the emblem, without the blue behind them.

**MMBN-2. MegaMan's status and money, in the real world.** Walking about with Lan in the real world, and in battles (MMBN-4; walking about on the net: the area's map, MMBN-6), the second screen shows the PET's MegaMan screen (START, MegaMan) and its money counter (START's menu, its top right corner) in one frame:
- at the top right, the money counter as the game draws it, without its own orange frame (its dark band and the amount, at the same place across as in the game), its lighter grey behind the amount made the dark grey-blue of the panel's top row (behind "MegaMan.exe" and the level), and the rest of its rows, left of it, filled with that colour too, so the counter's row and the panel read as one piece;
- right below it, with no gap, the MegaMan screen's panel as the game draws it, the whole width of the screen: MegaMan.exe and his level, his HP as it is now (the game refills it after each battle, in part only while the PET's battery is low), his armor when he has one, and his buster's Attack, Rapid and Charge. The green arrow beside the armor (the screen's cursor) is left out, the screen's own grid there; so is the screen's message box below the panel (its question to the player, "Boost which feature?", with MegaMan's face);
- above the two and below them, the money counter's orange edge (its dark outline outside, its bright glow in the middle of the screen), as wide as the screen, made from the counter's own top and bottom edges; none at the sides, which would cut into the panel;
- the whole frame centred top to bottom on the screen, black above and below it.

(In the first test build: the panel alone at the top, without the money; in the second, the counter kept its own lighter grey and had black left of it; in the third, whatever was behind the counter in the game (the net's paths, a Navi walking by) showed through it in its lighter grey now and then: it's taken without it since.) They're opened in the background on a silent copy of the game about once a second, and at once when MegaMan's HP or its maximum, the area or the money changes (back from a battle, an HPMemory, another area, Mystery Data, a shop). Your game is never paused. The counter is as the game draws it with nothing behind it: the net's paths or a Navi walking behind it in the game never show through it.

**MMBN-3. Elsewhere, the last picture.** In conversations and scenes, in the PET's own menus (the MegaMan screen open in the game too), shops and the chip trader, the second screen keeps the last picture: MegaMan's status (MMBN-2) in the real world, the area's map (MMBN-6) on the net. Back from a battle, the status is taken again at once (his HP has changed) and the map made again.

**MMBN-4. Battles: on the game screen; MegaMan's status on the second.** A battle plays on the game screen as the game draws it: the field, MegaMan's HP, the custom gauge, the custom screen with its chips and cursor, the tutorials' messages and arrows, and the results. The second screen shows MegaMan's status and money (MMBN-2) as they were when the battle began, on the net too (in place of the map): his HP there is the HP he went in with, his HP in the battle being the game's own, on the game screen; it's taken again as the battle ends. A battle with no status taken before it (a save state loaded in a battle) shows the logo. (Until the seventh test build with maps, the custom screen went to the second screen, on its own, while it was up, and the second screen showed the logo for the rest of a battle.)

**MMBN-5. One screen.** With one screen, the game plays as it is.

**MMBN-6. On the net, the area's map.** Walking about on the net, the second screen shows a map of the area MegaMan is in, centred on MegaMan and following him as he walks, but stopping at the area's edges: near an edge the map stays put rather than show the black beyond the area's last paths and panels, and an area smaller than the screen sits in its middle (in the fourth test build with maps it was centred where the game screen was, leaving black beyond the edges): the area's paths and panels as the game draws them, at a third of their size, on black. The game has no map, so GBA DS draws one from the area's own layout. While MegaMan is in an area, the game holds the layout of the whole area (which of its tiles goes where), though it only ever draws the part around him. A silent copy of the game draws the rest the same way, with the game's own tiles and colours, as the GBA would if its screen were as big as the area. It draws only the paths and panels. Left out: the net's backdrop behind them (its sky, or a homepage's patterned floor); Navis, viruses, Mystery Data and other sprites; anything that animates. Where MegaMan is, his head marks the spot, at the game's own size (not a third): his standing sprite facing the camera as the game draws it, cut off at his chin, taken from the same copy when the map is made (so in his armour's colours). It never animates or turns, and only moves with him. Nothing else is added: no names, no frame. (Without the marker until the sixth test build with maps. In the sixth, an area entered on a raised floor, as WWW Comp 5 from its entrance pad when jacking in, had no map, and the head sat below him wherever he stood higher up.) The map is whole as soon as MegaMan arrives in an area, parts he can't reach yet included, and is made again each time he does: half a second after he arrives, or once he has stepped off the warp pad he arrived on (the logo until then, about two seconds). It stays up in conversations and the PET's menus on the net; in battles, MegaMan's status instead (MMBN-4), and the map again after. (In the first three test builds with maps, a copy walked MegaMan everywhere he could go and pieced the map together from what the game showed, taking up to a minute an area on the device and missing raised floors; in the third, a map started during a warp failed and stayed failed.) *By hand: how the map looks on the device. In exact runs the maps are drawn as well, so the tests cover them.*

## Exceptions

- **MegaMan's status and money in one frame (MMBN-2).** The game shows them on two screens of the PET, the money in a small box on START's menu, the status on the MegaMan screen. On the second screen they're put together, the money counter's own orange frame taken off it and drawn again, from its own top and bottom edges, across the screen above and below both. The counter and the panel are as the game draws them, but for the counter's background, made the panel's top row's colour and carried across the counter's row; only the frame and that fill are new, and they're the game's own colours, so the whole still reads as the PET's. As Final Fantasy Tactics Advance's turn order and Laws (FT-9).

- **MegaMan's head on the net's maps (MMBN-6).** Principle 2 keeps a controlled character off the second screen. Once the map stopped at the area's edges, MegaMan was no longer always in its middle, so the owner wanted to see where he is (Nov 10, 2026). It's kept a marker, not a second MegaMan to steer: only his head, at the game's own size over a map at a third of it (as other GBA games' maps mark the player), from his standing sprite facing the camera, never animated or turned. It's the game's own sprite, cut at his chin.
- **The net's maps, drawn from each area's layout (MMBN-6).** This goes against three principles:
  - principle 1, because the map is the game's pictures at a third of their size, not GBA pixels;
  - principles 3 and 4, because it's a screen the game never draws, put together by GBA DS from the game's memory, which GBA DS doesn't do anywhere else.

  It earns its place because the net is a maze of look-alike paths and the game gives you no map of it. Players have long made their own maps from the same tiles.

  It's kept as close to the game as it can be:
  - only the game's own tiles and colours, placed by the layout the game itself holds for the area;
  - drawn by the GBA's own graphics hardware, on a copy of the player's own game;
  - nothing new on it, and never shipped (GBA DS ships no art).

  The first way tried kept to screens the game draws: a copy walked MegaMan everywhere he could go and pieced together what the game showed. It took up to a minute an area on the device, missed raised floors, and caught a homepage's backdrop around the edges (Nov 9–10, 2026). The owner approved this one (Nov 10, 2026).

## Not covered yet

Tried on three endgame saves from GameFAQs (two USA, one European: walking about on the net and in Lan's room, a conversation, the PET's menus, a battle with its custom screen, its results and back), the first tutorial battle's custom screen (the owner's states: the teacher's message, her arrows over the viruses) and the first minutes of a new game in the USA release (Lan in his room before he has his PET: START opens nothing, the logo stays). Not tried: the tutorial battles' arrows pointing into the custom screen (they'd go with it), the later tutorials, boss battles, the ElecMan chapter's PET battery (HP only partly refilled after a battle), net battles over a link cable, the final battle, the credits, and the Japanese release beyond its title and the start of a new game (its RAM is at the USA's addresses there).

## Facts found so far (for sessions)

The disassembly at github.com/Silenthal/bn1 (USA) names the RAM (its `ld_script.ld`) and the code; the European and Japanese releases have the same addresses.
