What GBA DS does with these games. Each rule is a promise the profile keeps; the regression tests check them (the cases citing a rule are listed by `tools/regress.py --list pokemon`). Change a rule here first when the behaviour is meant to change, then the profile, then the tests and goldens. Rule numbers are never reused: a dropped rule stays, marked *retired*.

Every game also follows the [general rules](README.md#general-rules) (GEN-1 to GEN-13).

The five games share one engine (FireRed and LeafGreen's, Ruby and Sapphire's, Emerald's) and every release in every language is handled the same way; the rules hold for all 30 release codes above. Romhacks built on them (PK-7) are the reason the profile finds things on the screen and in the Pokémon's own data rather than at addresses.

## Why these screens

Asked what a Pokémon game would have put on a second GBA screen: the two screens a player keeps opening START for are the **Party** (who is hurt, asleep, poisoned, how much HP is left) and the **map** (where am I, where is that town). Both are screens the game already draws. They're opened on the hidden copy and shown as the game draws them, so nothing is rebuilt or invented, and the game screen is never covered. There is no trainer card, no Pokédex and no summary: nothing that would need a cursor or a choice (the Party is shown with no Pokémon lit, and the game's own prompt and CANCEL button are left out).

## Rules

**PK-1. Logo.** Whenever the second screen has no Party to show (PK-2), it shows the logo: on the title and the menu, in a new game before the first Pokémon, in the intro and in scenes, and in a fight, when the Party hasn't been shown yet. It's the game's own title logo (the lettering and its version name, without PRESS START and the copyright line), taken from this ROM's own title screen. A romhack has its own (PK-7).

**PK-2. Party.** With Pokémon in the party and the START menu open to the player, the second screen shows the game's Party screen, filling the screen, with all six slots as the game draws them (HP bars, levels, status, held items), the cursor on no Pokémon and without the "Choose a Pokémon." box or CANCEL. Once shown it stays through scenes and fights (the game's START menu can't be opened in them), as it was last taken, until it can be taken again (PK-6). It's taken again when anything about the party changes (a Pokémon's HP, status, level or species), within a second or two of the change.

**PK-3. Map.** After the Party it shows the game's region map: in FireRed and LeafGreen the Town Map (a key item the player gets early on), in Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald the Pokénav's region map (once the player has the Pokénav), as the game draws them with the player's place marked, without the button prompts. Before the player has it, only the Party is shown. The map is taken again about every 15 seconds, so the marker can lag behind a player who has just walked into a new town (known issue). Before the player has the map, the second screen looks for it every few seconds without slowing the Party down: the Bag's Key Items list is compared with how it was when there was none, and only a changed list is searched, so the map turns up within seconds of the player getting it. The Party is on the second screen as soon as the game is, never held back by the map's first search (the logo shows only until the Party is taken, a second or so); the first start of a ROM file shows an "Initializing game..." screen while its checksum is read and its logo extracted, so that none of that slows the game after it starts; the map follows when it is found, within seconds, and the search is short: in FireRed and LeafGreen the Town Map's row in the Key Items list is read from the game's memory, and only a romhack that keeps its Bag differently is searched row by row. In FireRed and LeafGreen the Town Map is found wherever it sits in the Key Items list (in real saves it is often a dozen rows down, after the Bicycle, the Teachy TV and the passes), not only near the top. In Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald the map is always the full view: the Pokénav opens it in whichever view the player last left it, and when that is the zoom view the hidden copy switches it to the full view (A) before taking it, so the whole region shows. The end of the FireRed and LeafGreen Town Map's "GUIDE" text on the blue strip is painted over (it is cut off at the screen's edge). The Party's background is redrawn below the last Pokémon, so no leftover of the game's message box shows (the bottom rows of Ruby's and Sapphire's Party screen).

**PK-3a. Each game its own map.** What the second screen knows about a game (its map, where its Town Map or Pokénav is, where its party is) is kept for that ROM only. Starting another Pokémon game, or loading a state of one, never shows a map from the game played before, nor a "no map" found there: a game without a map yet (Super MarioMon, a new game) shows only the Party until it has its own, and FireRed shows its own Town Map, not the previous game's, within a few seconds of starting.

**PK-4. Turning pages.** With both, the second screen turns from one to the other every 5 seconds, the way Fire Emblem's status screen turns through its pages (FE-3), except in a battle: there it stays on the Party and doesn't turn to the map (the map comes back when the battle is over). A battle is told by the game's display setup (the sprite window it draws with, which nothing else in these games uses); checked on Super MarioMon's wild and tutorial battles, and on the other games' battles by hand (*By hand: a battle in FireRed, LeafGreen, Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald: the second screen stays on the Party; if the map shows in a fight, send a bug report*).

**PK-5. Game screen untouched.** The START menu, the Bag, scenes and fights stay on the game screen where the game draws them; nothing the second screen shows is taken from it, and opening the Party or the map on the hidden copy changes nothing the player sees or hears. (GEN-2.)

**PK-6. Menus that aren't the original's.** The Bag and the Pokénav are found by trying each row of the START menu and telling the screen that opens by the game's display registers, not by their place in the list; a menu without SAVE or EXIT (Radical Red, Rogue), with extra entries, or that is a bar of icons along the bottom (Unbound) works the same. A Bag whose pockets wrap round (FRLG+: Right from the last pocket comes back to the first, and the Key Items pocket isn't second from the left) is found out by pressing Right until a pocket's tab comes back; the Town Map is then looked for in each pocket in turn, and the pocket it was found in (known by its tab) is tried first next time. A START menu that can't be found, or a Bag with no Town Map, leaves the logo (PK-1) or just the Party (PK-3). When the player already has the START menu or the Party screen up, the hidden copy backs out of it first (B) and opens its own, so the second screen catches up without waiting for the player to be back on the map. Where the START menu can't be opened at all (a conversation, a scene, a shop), the second screen tries again every second, not after longer and longer waits, so it catches up the moment the player is back on the map.

**PK-7. Romhacks, as separate ROMs and as patches.** A romhack built on the original ROM (Radical Red, Unbound, Gaia, Saiph, Fuligin, FRLG+, Light Platinum, Flora Sky, Glazed, Theta Emerald EX are the ones checked, in the Mods row) shows the Party and the map as the game it's built on does (PK-2 to PK-4), as a ROM of its own or as a patch on the original ROM (GEN-9). A separate ROM has a logo of its own, taken from its own title screen and kept for that ROM file (so hacks of the same game don't share one); a patched game is the original ROM file and shows the original's logo, and none is taken while a patch is on. Super MarioMon and Emerald Rogue EX are rebuilt from source (nothing like Emerald's ROM, so only ROMs of their own, PK-8). FRLG+ 1.5.1 (a BPS patch for the USA / Europe FireRed, tested as a ROM of its own and as a patch file) shows its Party and its Town Map (found in the Key Items pocket of its wrapping Bag, PK-6). Known issue: Gaia's Party screen has black vertical bars of its own.

**PK-8. Super MarioMon and Emerald Rogue EX.** Both are Emerald rebuilt from its source, with their own words (Super MarioMon: "Captures", "Pockets", "Mario"; its Pokémon are Mario's captures) and their own title logos (PK-7). Super MarioMon: the logo on the title, in the bedroom before the first capture, in the tutorial battle and in the first wild fights before there is a Party; the Party from the first control with a capture (there is no map in it yet); the Party kept while the Captures screen is up and through a wild battle. Emerald Rogue EX: the Party (its Pokémon screen has its own colours) and, once the player has it, its map like Emerald's (the owner's device test, Nov 20, 2026: it draws one); the logo before the first Pokémon. Checked on real play states (not poked ones) sent from the device.

## Real saves (for building states)

Checked with the owner's collection (Nov 19, 2026); `tools/pokemon/saves.py` does the conversions and makes the states (`info`, `clean`, `frlg-jp`, `state`).

- **One save, every language.** A save from one language loads unchanged on every other language's ROM of the same game and on every Rev: the game shows the save's text as it is and its own place names (a Cinnabar Island save is Zinnoberinsel on the German ROM). Ruby and Sapphire saves load on each other's ROMs; so do FireRed's and LeafGreen's. Japanese Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald take international saves too. 43 releases have a mid-game state made this way (`states/pokemon/mid/`, cases `pk-mid-*`).
- **Japanese FireRed and LeafGreen** treat an international save as no save (the new-game screens come up) unless the 80 bytes at the end of save section 4's data (offset 3768, a table of 12-byte records) are zeroed, as they are in a Japanese save: `saves.py frlg-jp`. A Japanese save loads on the international ROMs as it is.
- **Not across games.** An Emerald save on Ruby or Sapphire: "the save file has been corrupted". A Ruby or Sapphire save on Emerald loads but half-read (no Pokédex, no badges, a place that isn't there): not usable.
- **Romhacks.** Fuligin takes FireRed and LeafGreen saves, Flora Sky Emerald saves; Light Platinum and Super MarioMon load a vanilla save at a place and with Pokémon that don't exist in them; Radical Red, Gaia, Saiph and Rogue EX start a new game with one; Unbound, Glazed and Theta stayed on their title or a blank screen with a save made here (sixteen presses of A, one try each: not looked into further), so their states are the owner's own, played on the device. Every state has to be played (or a new game with the party written into it, tools/pokemon/README.md).
- **Old emulator saves** (`.sps`, `.gsv`) of 64 KB have no second slot; `saves.py clean` makes one (otherwise: "the save file is corrupted", then it loads). The saves from a memory-card collection (player "MEMORY", 999:59, every Pokédex entry) are real FireRed, LeafGreen, Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald layouts whatever the file's header says.
- Where the Town Map sits in the Key Items list varies a lot (row 0 in a short game, row 10 or 13 in the owner's saves): PK-3 finds it wherever it is.

## Not covered yet

- A battle's party screen (START isn't available in a fight, so the logo stays; the game's party screen in a fight could be taken another way).
- Real saves for the Japanese Rev 1 releases, the Pokémon Box and debug dumps, the Switch Online dumps and a Korean release (no ROM). Every other release has a mid-game state from a real save (see below); the romhacks have states from real play or a real save (Radical Red, Super MarioMon, Rogue EX, Unbound, Glazed, Theta, Fuligin, Saiph, Flora Sky) except Gaia and Light Platinum, which have a ROM in the fixtures and no state yet.
- Super MarioMon's map (it hasn't shown one), and the rest of both hacks (what a player meets later on). A map for Unbound, Glazed, Theta, Fuligin and Flora Sky (their states are before the Town Map), and any state for Gaia and Light Platinum.
- The Japanese Rev 1 releases, the Pokémon Box and debug dumps, the Switch Online dumps and any Korean release (no fixture).
- The map's refresh (PK-3) only after a few captures.
- **Tested on the device (the owner, Nov 20, 2026, BananaOS on the RG DS Plus, side build 1.5.7-dev+pokemon): all the games and romhacks in this file count as tested, and every one draws its map except Saiph and Super MarioMon;** the vanilla releases draw the Party and its map; the Party stays in a battle (cases `pk-battle-*`: his own wild-battle states of the five USA/Europe games); Unbound, Glazed and Theta Emerald EX draw the Party; Rogue EX draws its map. No freeze or pause outside a deliberate loading screen: the start-up pause was RetroAchievements loading a game's set on the game's thread, now behind "Loading achievements..." (FireRed and LeafGreen USA/Europe, 61 achievements); the "Initializing game..." screen was never seen in his testing (the checksums and logos were already known).
- **Known issues:** Saiph and Super MarioMon show only the Party, no map: Saiph's Bag is searched row by row (about 7 s on a PC, more on the device) and its Town Map hasn't turned up on the device; MarioMon has no Town Map or Pokénav the capture knows. Both are romhacks, so they stay known issues for now. Gaia's picture had black bars when tested on a PC; not re-checked. Unbound, Glazed and Theta Emerald EX have cases (`pk-unbound`, `pk-glazed`, `pk-theta`; also `pk-fuligin`, `pk-saiph`, `pk-florasky`) on the owner's own early-game states from the device (Piplup, Larvitar, and Birch's intro); none of them has a Town Map yet, so the map isn't checked.
