## Romhacks and patched ROMs

GBA DS knows a game by its code, which romhacks and patches of a supported game usually keep, so they start with the game's second screen. Whether it works depends on what the mod changes: new text, items, stats and colours are usually fine, while a mod that changes the screens GBA DS shows (a redrawn menu or map) can leave the second screen wrong or blank.

- **Tested romhacks and patches** (colour restorations, voice removals...) are in the table's last column, on the row of the game they're made from, with what's different in the game's section. GBA DS knows them by their checksum, and they're tested like the games. They aren't counted as games of their own.
- **Any other patched or modified ROM** runs with the game's second screen, untested. The game list says so under the game's logo ("Romhack: ..." for one GBA DS knows, "Modified ROM" for any other), once it has read the ROM the first time you select it, and bug reports say the same, so if the second screen goes wrong in a mod, send one. To ask for a romhack to be supported, open an issue with a link to it.
- **Patches GBA DS knows:** besides the tested ones, GBA DS knows a list of colour, voice and translation patches for other games too (some not supported yet), so the Patches menu can name them and say when one is made for another version of the game (a `.ips` file can't say which version it's for, so this is the only warning you'll get).
- **Patch files:** put the patch (`.ips`, `.ups`, `.bps` or `.xdelta`) in the game's folder in GBA DS's `patches` folder (GBA DS makes it, named after the game: `patches/Mario & Luigi - Superstar Saga (USA)`) and switch it on in the pause menu's Extras → Patches, which says whether it's a tested romhack (see [Patches](Playing#patches)). A ROM you've already patched works too.
- Save states made on the unchanged game shouldn't be loaded into a mod of it, or the other way round.
- A romhack with its own title screen still shows the original game's logo on the second screen.

## How the maps work

GBA DS runs a second, silent copy of the game next to the one you're playing. About every second (`map_interval` in `gba-ds.cfg`), and right after each room transition, it copies your game's state into the silent copy, presses the map button there, waits for the map screen, and records a short loop of frames so blinking markers still blink. Before showing a map it checks two things: a memory flag that's only set while the map screen is open, and fixed pixels of the map screen itself. Title screens, cutscenes, menus and fades are rejected, and the last good map stays up. Your game is never paused or touched.

You see the game's own map, with everything it shows (explored rooms, save and warp rooms, completion, area names), at sharp 4× scale on the RG DS Plus.

## Advance Wars and Advance Wars 2: Black Hole Rising

The USA and European releases of both, and in Japan Game Boy Wars Advance 1+2, which has both games on one cartridge. The two work the same way.

- **In a battle, on your turn,** the second screen shows the **intel of the unit under the cursor** (the screen R opens, with its text box, where the game puts them), yours or the enemy's (yours stays while you move it and pick its action), or else the **Battle Intel** (bases and funds) in the middle. Both are brought up in the background from the menus of a silent copy of the game, as the other games' maps are; your game is never paused.
- Nothing leaves the game screen: the **CO and day plate**, the **terrain and unit info**, the DAMAGE box and **conversations** stay where the game draws them.
- **On the computer's turn** (from the moment you end yours, through its moves) the second screen shows the logo: the cursor isn't yours, so there's no intel to bring up. Your army is taken to be the first, as in the campaigns; in Versus, if you play a later army or two people share the device, those turns show the logo too.
- **In a fight,** on the title and menus, and on a battle's results pages, the second screen shows the game's logo.
- **Known issue (won't fix):** in Advance Wars' **Field Training** missions the second screen keeps the logo: the tutorial blocks the menus it's built from (SELECT and START). It works fully in Campaign and Versus.
- **Game Boy Wars Advance 1+2** (Japan): GBA DS notices which of the two games you pick from the cartridge's menu and sets the second screen up as for that game (Game Boy Wars Advance as Advance Wars, Game Boy Wars Advance 2 as Advance Wars 2). On the cartridge's own title and menu, and on both games' titles, the second screen shows the cartridge's logo. Both games have been tried in battles (Game Boy Wars Advance's unit intel and the computer's turn too); Game Boy Wars Advance 2's computer's turn hasn't been seen yet.

## Baldur's Gate: Dark Alliance

The USA and European releases.

The game has no map, so GBA DS makes one. Each time you arrive in an area, a copy of the game, running silently beside yours, looks over the whole area a screen at a time; the second screen then shows the area's floors, walls and furniture at a quarter of their size, following you and stopping at the area's edges, with a red X where you are: the one the game puts on an unusable quick slot. Rooms you haven't reached yet are on it too. Enemies, barrels and pots aren't. The logo shows on the title, while the map is being made, and at GAME OVER.

Known issues: making a big area's map takes up to about 2 seconds on the RG DS Plus, and the game pauses for that moment as you arrive; doors you open aren't shown open until you come back to the area. Eldrith's Watch, the last boss's arena, is shown on the game's blue background colour, as the scenery behind it isn't part of the map.

## Banjo-Kazooie: Grunty's Revenge

The USA and Europe release, Europe's English, French and German release, and the Italian and Spanish releases.

## Boktai: The Sun Is in Your Hand

The USA, European and Japanese (Bokura no Taiyou) releases.

- **While you walk about,** the second screen shows the game's **Map** (the SELECT menu's Map page, without its title bar and button prompts), following Django. It is taken from a hidden copy of the game about once a second.
- **At the top** it has the game's own **clock** (top left) and the **sunrise** and **sunset** times (top right), the lines the ITEM page shows (START there switches between them), looked at about every 30 seconds, so they can lag a little behind the game.
- In conversations, scenes and the menus, the last Map stays. At the title, on the file menu and at the first start's set-up screens the second screen shows the logo: the title's lettering.
- **The sun sensor:** the cartridge's solar sensor reads 6 of 10 to begin with, so the sun gauge isn't empty (`solar_level` in `gba-ds.cfg` sets that, from 0 to 10). Swipe up from the bottom of the touchscreen for the quick bar: its **Sun** row has **Dark**, **-**, **+** and **Full** to change the sunlight while you play, as RetroArch's sun sensor buttons do (for the ice dungeons, where shading the sensor keeps the ice from melting). The level is kept for the game.
- Known: the Europe and Japan releases were checked on their logos and by start-up only. Not tried yet: bosses, the Solar Station and the World map.

## Breath of Fire

The USA, European and Japanese (Ryuu no Senshi) releases, and Europe's English, French and German release.

- **On the overworld** the second screen is the **world map** START opens (the cyan marker is where the party is), once the party has the Map item; it is taken from a hidden copy of the game about once a second, so your game isn't touched. Until the party has the Map, it shows the party overview instead.
- **Everywhere else** (towns, caves, castles) it shows the first page of SELECT's menu as a **party overview**: the place, the gold, and each character's level, HP and AP, without the command list.
- **In a battle** it shows the party overview from before the fight, also when the map was up as it began; the numbers on it are those from before the battle (the battle's own boxes on the game screen are live). This is one of the exceptions on the [Philosophy](https://github.com/kyannon/gba-ds/wiki/Philosophy) page.
- With the menu, one of its pages or the map open on the game screen, the second screen shows the overview, not the same thing twice. On the title it shows the logo: the title's lettering. In conversations and shops the last picture stays.
- Known: Europe's English, French and German release was tried at its title only (its saves are of another kind than the ones tested); cutscenes that share the title's screen mode may show the logo until they end; the battle's HP and AP boxes stay on the game screen.

## Crash Nitro Kart

The HUD is moved off the game screen rather than copied, as in Mario Kart: Super Circuit.

- **Driving** (races, Time Trial and the Adventure hub):
  - The game screen shows the track and the karts, with the **item** you hold (top centre) and your **place** (bottom left) where the game draws them, and the turn signs and the countdown.
  - The second screen has the time, wumpa fruit and lap along the top (on the hub, your trophies and keys), the **top-four faces** with their places down the left, and the **course map** with every racer's dot, twice as big on every course.
  - Behind it all is a dimmed picture of the course's own sky and scenery, captured once each race has faded in. (On the hub, whose sky is a plain colour, it's mostly dark.)

- The game's **pause menu** stays on the game screen, with the last HUD on the second screen. TRY AGAIN, the title and the menus show the logo.
- With one screen (`layout = single`), the HUD stays where the game draws it.
- Not tried yet: Adventure's boss races, CNK challenges, relic races and crystal arenas, Time Trial's results, and link play.
